Why ordinary search isn’t enough

You simply don’t see some of the jobs that fit

A blind spotMany employers post jobs only on their own sites — they never reach the big job boards. A job that fits exists, but you can’t see it where you’re used to looking.

You can’t cover it by handTo avoid missing such jobs, you’d have to know dozens of companies, visit their pages regularly and keep it all in your head. That’s hard to keep up for long.

A cluttered feedThe big job boards’ interfaces are overloaded, the same jobs repeat, and the source and freshness of a listing are hard to make out.

Everything is scatteredApplications, notes and the history of requirements are spread across spreadsheets and bookmarks. Pulling it all together before an interview is a job in itself.

How it works

From the first job to the interview result

Four scenarios the product is built for — all in one clear interface.

A
First search

Results right away, not a blank screen

Set your region, specialization, the technologies you need and the work format — and see current jobs right away, from a saved and automatically growing list of employers. Among them will be jobs from career pages. Companies that fit can be added to monitoring.

B
Subscription and monitoring

Company monitoring — always a current picture

Save a search and pick the companies you care about. From then on the app, on a schedule you set, looks for jobs according to your filters, grows the list of employers and shows new and changed jobs — with source, update date, salary and language requirements.

C
Analysis and preparation

Clear what to brush up and what to show

Study the requirements and how they changed, match the technologies and experience a job asks for against your own skills, and prepare your CV and answers for a specific employer. Saved jobs are easy to compare side by side.

D
Applying and tracking

Every step of the search — in one place

You send the CV yourself, and in the app you record the hiring stages, notes, dates and interview outcomes. When you apply, a copy of the job is saved with its requirements and salary as of that date. It stays available even if the listing is changed or taken down.

Works without you

Scheduled auto-search

Set a schedule — and the app looks for jobs according to your filters on its own: daily, on weekdays or however suits you. It scans the connected sites, flags new and changed jobs and brings only what actually changed — no searching from scratch.

  • You set how often: how frequently and when to search — to your rhythm.
  • The search follows your saved filters and chosen companies exactly.
  • The result is a summary of new and changed jobs, without the noise.

Source and freshness

See where a job came from and when it was checked

For every job the essentials are visible: the source, where it was found, when it first appeared and when it was last checked. A failed check doesn’t mark a job as closed — it’s marked closed only when that’s confirmed.

  • Where the job was found: on a career page, on a job board, on both, or not checked yet.
  • A history of changes to requirements, salary and terms — from the very start.
  • A freshness mark: “checked today” or “not seen for N days”.

Salary and languages

Salary and languages — exactly as in the job

Salary is shown exactly as stated in the job: amount or range, currency, period, before or after tax. The lower-bound filter compares only comparable figures; the rest is flagged separately.

  • If the salary isn’t stated, it says so — that’s not zero and not a guess.
  • For language, you see separately: whether a language is stated and whether it’s required or only preferred.
  • The “English is fine” mark appears only when the job clearly implies it.

Good to know

Questions that matter

What does “job only on the company site” mean?

It’s a job found on a company’s career page but not found on the connected job boards when checked. That mark is set only after a full check of the boards; if a board was unavailable, it shows “not checked” with a reason. The result is phrased as “not found on the boards when checked on such-and-such date,” not “not on the market.”

Do you promise every job on the market?

No — and we say so plainly. roleorienta covers the main boards and companies on common hiring systems, and states clearly what is and isn’t covered. Collection happens only from open pages and permitted sources; getting around access restrictions and CAPTCHAs is not part of the product.

Where does the data come from and how current is it?

Every job shows its source (a board or a company site), the date it was first found and the date it was last checked, plus a freshness mark. Change history is kept from the very start, so you always see what changed and when.

How does company monitoring work?

You choose the employers you care about, and the app regularly checks their jobs and grows the list. New and changed jobs arrive as in-app notifications and in a regular summary that shows changes, not sheer volume.

How often does the app look for new jobs?

You set the frequency yourself. In auto-search mode the app, on a schedule you set — for example, every weekday morning — looks for jobs according to your filters and chosen companies, then sends a summary: what’s new and what changed. No need to search again by hand.

Do you send applications for me?

No. You always send the application yourself — the app only helps you prepare and keep the history. Automatically sending applications or messages to employers is not part of the product.

How does the app work out a company’s technologies?

The app gathers every job it found at the employer and combines the technologies into one profile of three groups: what appears most often, what a specific job requires, and what occurs at the company but not in this job. Identical jobs (duplicates and translations) are counted once. It always shows how many jobs the profile is based on, and you can jump from any item to the source jobs.

What about salary and languages?

Salary is shown as stated in the job: amount or range, currency, period, before or after tax. The filter compares only comparable figures. For language, you see separately: whether a language is stated and whether it’s required or only preferred; “not stated” never becomes “not needed.”

Are my applications and notes private?

Yes. One user’s data isn’t accessible to another. Messages from users — error reports and company suggestions — don’t change data automatically: an administrator reviews them first.
