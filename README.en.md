# roleorienta

**An app for searching, monitoring and analyzing jobs: see what isn't on the big job boards, and run your whole search in one place.**

roleorienta brings together jobs from the big job boards and companies' own career pages, tracks changes at the employers you choose, and turns the collected data into concrete preparation — from the first job to the interview result.

Next to this description sits a visual presentation page (`roleorienta-en.html`) — open it in a browser to see the job cards, the source details and the preparation flow at a glance.

---

## The problem

In many job markets a meaningful share of interesting jobs are published **only on companies' own sites** and never reach the big job boards. For the candidate this is a blind spot: jobs that fit exist, but they aren't visible where you're used to looking.

Checking dozens of career pages by hand is hard: you have to know which companies to watch, return to them regularly, notice changes in requirements and salary, and never lose the details. The big job boards don't do this systematically, and their results are often overloaded: the same jobs repeat, with no clear source or update date.

---

## Who it's for

roleorienta suits an **active candidate** searching in a specific professional niche and region who wants to:

- see every job that fits, including those not found on the big job boards;
- follow companies of interest and notice changes in their jobs;
- understand which technologies and experience an employer needs, and prepare for a specific company;
- keep applications and interviews in one place, not in spreadsheets and bookmarks.

The specific niche and market are set during configuration — the product doesn't depend on them. A second role is the **administrator (source curator)**: they confirm uncertainly detected sources, handle error reports and maintain the list of employers.

---

## How it works

The path from the first job to the interview result is four scenarios, and they all live in one interface:

1. **First search.** Set your region, specialization, the technologies you need and the work format — and see current jobs right away, not a blank screen: from a saved and automatically growing list of employers. Among the jobs will be ones from career pages. Companies that fit can be added to monitoring.
2. **Company monitoring.** Save a search and pick the companies you care about. From then on the app, on a schedule you set, looks for jobs according to your filters, grows the list of employers and shows new and changed jobs — with source, update date, salary and language requirements.
3. **Analysis and preparation.** Study the requirements and how they changed, match the technologies and experience a job asks for against your own skills, and prepare your CV and answers for a specific employer. Saved jobs are easy to compare side by side.
4. **Applying and tracking.** You send the CV yourself, and in the app you record the hiring stages, notes, dates and interview outcomes. When you apply, a copy of the job is saved with its requirements and salary as of that date. It stays available even if the listing is changed or taken down.

The app doesn't send applications or messages to employers automatically — you always apply yourself.

## What's inside

**Scheduled auto-search.** The key feature: on a schedule you set — daily, on weekdays or however suits you — the app looks for jobs on its own, according to your saved filters and chosen companies. It flags new and changed jobs and sends a summary of only what actually changed — no searching from scratch by hand.

**Collection and coverage.** Jobs are gathered from two kinds of source — the big job boards and companies' own career pages. For every job you can see where it was found: on a job board, only on the company site, on both, or not checked yet. New employers are discovered continuously — both manually and automatically — and a source that's detected uncertainly is sent to a person for confirmation rather than connected blindly.

**Source and freshness.** For every job you can see the source and the date it was last checked: when it was first found and when it was checked most recently. If something isn't stated, it says so — the app doesn't make things up for the source. Change history is kept from the very start — you can see what changed and when in the requirements, salary and terms. A failed check doesn't close a job: it's marked closed only when that's confirmed.

**Search, filters and subscriptions.** Filters by region, role, level, technologies, work format, company and source, salary and language requirements, and by where the job was found. You decide whether to include jobs with empty fields. Saved searches and the companies you've picked to monitor are the heart of the monitoring scenario.

**Salary and languages — as in the job.** Salary is shown exactly as stated in the job: amount or range, currency, period, before or after tax. The lower-bound filter compares only comparable figures; the rest is flagged separately. For language you see separately whether a language is stated and whether it's required or only preferred; "not stated" never becomes "not needed."

**Analysis and preparation.** A job's requirements with links to the original wording, a profile of your skills and its comparison against the job's requirements, and the employer's technology profile (what appears most often / what a specific job needs / what occurs at the company but not in this job). You can see how many jobs it's based on, and jump from any item to those jobs. Several saved jobs can be compared side by side.

**Applications and interviews.** An application is saved together with a copy of the job as it was when you applied. Hiring stages, notes, contacts, dates and interview outcomes — all in one place. Even if the original job is taken down, the application and the saved copy remain.

**Notifications and summaries.** In-app notifications about new jobs and notable changes in saved ones, plus a regular summary (in the app and by email) that shows changes rather than sheer volume.

**Roles and privacy.** Two roles — user and administrator (curator). One user's data isn't accessible to another. Messages from users (error reports, company suggestions) don't change data automatically — an administrator reviews them first.

## Why it helps

Ordinary search makes you crawl sites by hand, trust an overloaded feed with no clear source, and keep the whole history in your head. roleorienta removes that grind: it shows jobs from career pages alongside the usual results, shows the source and check date for each job, tracks changes at employers, and pulls everything — from requirements to interviews — into one clear picture.

Fewer blind spots. More trust in the data. And a ready basis for preparing for each company.

---

## Coverage boundaries

We don't promise full coverage of every job on the market, and we say so plainly. Collection happens only from open pages and permitted sources; getting around access restrictions and CAPTCHAs is not part of the product. Automatic sending of applications is not supported — the decision and the action are always the candidate's.
